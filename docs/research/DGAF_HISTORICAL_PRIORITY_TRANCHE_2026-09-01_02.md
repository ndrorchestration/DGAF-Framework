# DGAF Historical-Priority Audit — Tranche 2026-09-01-02

**Status:** Narrow cross-domain lifecycle sweep completed; no absolute novelty or priority conclusion
**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Scope:** Pre-2026-04-29 and boundary-adjacent systems specifically tested against the remaining hypothesis that organizational/formation state itself becomes the governed object whose transition produces or authorizes a frozen software/research candidate whose evidence is independently verified against that same candidate.

## 1. Audit question

The remaining historical question is narrower than primitive novelty:

`formation state → formation authority → governed formation transition → evidence generation → exact candidate identity/freeze → candidate-bound verification → authorization`

A source is relevant as an exact predecessor only if the inspected primary material materially supports this continuous lifecycle with substantially the same governed-object boundary. Shared features alone are insufficient.

## 2. New comparator findings

### A. Barber & Martin — Dynamic Reorganization of Decision-Making Groups (2001)

ACM's primary publication record for *Dynamic Reorganization of Decision-Making Groups* states that organizational structures specify decision-making frameworks and authority, and that Adaptive Decision-Making Frameworks dynamically modify those frameworks at runtime by changing who makes decisions and who carries them out. The paper also reports experiments evaluating the dynamically reorganized systems.

Primary record:

- https://doi.org/10.1145/375735.376432

**Adjudication:** This materially strengthens the prior finding that dynamic formation/reorganization plus authority is longstanding external prior. The paper also demonstrates that organizational reconfiguration and experimental evaluation can coexist in one research architecture. However, the inspected record does not establish an immutable software/research candidate boundary, candidate-scoped cryptographic evidence, independent candidate verification, or authorization of a frozen candidate. It is therefore **external prior for GF/AF and a useful boundary-adjacent comparator, not an exact predecessor for Q**.

### B. Negotiating team formation using deep reinforcement learning (2020)

The 2020 *Artificial Intelligence* paper describes agents negotiating to form teams and evaluates the resulting team-formation mechanisms experimentally in both non-spatial and spatial environments.

Primary record:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2020.103356

**Adjudication:** This establishes another pre-DGAF composition of dynamic team formation and experimental evaluation. The experimental object is the team-formation mechanism itself; the inspected record does not establish a governed organizational state whose transition freezes an exact software/research candidate and then gates authorization using independently verified evidence. **Near/boundary-adjacent prior; not an exact Q predecessor.**

### C. TB-CSPN organizational agent architecture (2025)

The 2025 Springer publication describes dynamic topic-based group formation, explicit Supervisor/Consultant/Worker roles, threshold-driven membership adaptation, structured transitions, and traceability-oriented coordination. The paper presents dynamic group formation as part of a formal organizational architecture.

Primary record:

- https://doi.org/10.1007/s10791-025-09667-2

**Adjudication:** This remains a **strong near-composition prior**. It materially reduces the space in which a claim about combining dynamic formation, authority/supervision, role structure, validation, and traceability could be original. The inspected source still does not establish the complete DGAF candidate lifecycle: formation-state governance crossing into an exact immutable software/research candidate, candidate-bound evidence, independent verification, and final authorization.

### D. Clarus — Coordinating Autonomous Research Agents toward Web-Scale Scientific Collaboration (submitted 2026-06-29)

The arXiv record dated June 29, 2026 describes an open, auditable, attributable, resource-aware multi-phase research collaboration infrastructure. Its object model coordinates projects, agents, and resources; its workflow includes team formation, phases, tasks, artifacts/evidence, audit, attribution, and checkpoints.

Primary record:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30246

**Adjudication:** Clarus is a strong **later convergence comparator**, not prior art against an April/May 2026 DGAF chronology. It is nevertheless highly probative against any broad claim that the overall cross-domain direction was conceptually unique. It independently demonstrates convergence toward dynamic team formation plus auditable research artifacts/evidence plus lifecycle checkpoints. The inspected record does not by itself establish the DGAF-specific candidate-freeze/authorization semantics.

## 3. Negative-result significance

The sweep deliberately targeted the remaining high-information predicate rather than reopening primitive novelty questions. The sources above demonstrate several important partial compositions:

- dynamic organization + authority;
- dynamic team formation + experimental evaluation;
- dynamic group formation + hierarchical supervision + traceability;
- later research team formation + artifact/evidence collection + audit + checkpoints.

None of the inspected pre-2026-04-29 sources established the complete continuous lifecycle:

`formation state → formation authority → governed transition → exact candidate identity → independently verified candidate evidence → authorization`

This is a **bounded negative result**, not evidence of global absence.

## 4. Chronology correction retained

The repository chronology remains split into distinct milestones:

- **2026-04-29:** earliest currently located public repository evidence for the named DGAF formulation.
- **2026-05-01:** explicit formation-level authority conflict, sovereign veto, timeout escalation/blocking, and idempotent formation-wave replay are documented.
- **2026-08-21:** explicit development/candidate separation and exact candidate identity are documented.

Therefore the repository evidence does not establish that the complete Q composition existed before April 29. The May 1 formation semantics must not be back-projected into the April 29 identity evidence, and the August 21 candidate-governance semantics must not be back-projected into the April/May formation evidence.

## 6. Audit conclusion

The current historical-priority thesis should remain **OPEN**, but it should now be stated even more narrowly.

The strongest defensible formulation is:

> DGAF may represent an independently developed cross-domain integration in which established organizational formation governance is carried across a governed transition boundary into an exact experimental candidate, with candidate-scoped evidence, independent verification, and authorization participating in the same lifecycle.

This is a research hypothesis, not a novelty, firstness, patentability, or priority verdict.

The audit should not describe the candidate-bound mechanism itself as primitive novelty. SLSA/in-toto establish the general exact-object evidence-binding principle; the potentially distinctive question is its use as a governance transition between organizational formation and experimental candidate authorization.

**Open:**

- Historical equivalence of the complete continuous lifecycle across the formation-to-candidate boundary.

**Not established:**

- Global novelty.
- Firstness.
- Patentability.
- Historical priority of the complete Q architecture.
- Empirical superiority.
- Production superiority.
- PDMAL efficacy (empirical N remains 0).

## 8. Next search boundary

The next sweep should concentrate on primary-source implementation/formal records that explicitly connect **organizational-state transitions** to **artifact/research-candidate creation or promotion** and then bind **verification/authorization** to that same identity. General team formation, general provenance, ordinary CI/CD release engineering, and ordinary runtime policy enforcement should be treated as background prior unless the source crosses this boundary.
